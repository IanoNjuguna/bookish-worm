## The Landscape — and Where We Differ

We are not the first to put music on a blockchain, and we won't pretend otherwise. The difference is architectural, not rhetorical:

- **Edition marketplaces (Sound, Catalog, Zora)** run curator-gated drops on EVM chains, where gas volatility taxes every collection and metadata lives behind platform-specific indexers. Doba settles on Cardano's eUTXO model with predictable sub-dollar fees, distributes fixed 1,000-fraction pools through audited Plutus V3 validators, and writes CIP-60 music metadata that any Cardano indexer resolves without our involvement.
- **Royalty-share platforms (Royal, Opulous)** sell claims on future income — income the platform itself continues to administer. Doba sells ownership. Payment settles atomically inside the purchase transaction; there is no royalty administrator, no deferred distribution, no counterparty between fan and artist.
- **Decentralized streaming (Audius)** reproduces streaming economics — attention exchanged for token incentives — on new rails. Our thesis is the inverse: ownership economics, where a hundred collectors outfund a hundred thousand passive plays.
- **Direct-to-fan incumbents (Bandcamp)** take ~15%, pay nothing on resale, and keep the audience graph on their servers. Our fee is basis points in public validator source; secondary-sale royalties execute on-chain; the ledger — not our database — is the record of who owns what.
- **Distributors (DistroKid, TuneCore)** are pipes into the old system. We don't compete with them. We make the trip unnecessary.

## One-Line Positioning

For independent artists and the fans who fund them, **Doba Protocol** is the ownership layer for music — direct settlement, on-chain splits, and fan-held assets — unlike streaming platforms and NFT marketplaces that rent audiences, defer payouts, or sell claims instead of ownership.
